questions

before you start.

Is Tsundoku only for physical books?

No. Organize physical books, ebooks, audiobooks, and even books you've read but don't own — borrowed, library or lent.

Do I have to add my books one by one?

No. Search, scan the ISBN barcode, type one in by hand — or paste a whole list of titles, one per line, and add them all at once.

What about omnibus editions and story collections?

Tag a book as an omnibus and each part gets its own summary and tick. Tag it as a collection and the list of stories appears automatically, so you can tick off a story at a time.

Do I have to track my reading?

Never. The reading log is off by default. Switch it on if you like seeing your pages and days; switch it off and it disappears. There are no streaks and no goals either way.

What are “my lists” for?

They're for books you don't own yet — a buy list, a borrow list, gift ideas. Tick a book off when you get it and it moves onto your shelf, so you stop buying duplicates.

Can I share my library?

Yes. Make your shelf public and share one link. Friends can browse and search it without an account.

Why is it called Tsundoku?

Tsundoku is a Japanese word for buying books and letting them pile up before reading them. We think that's part of being a reader — not something to feel guilty about.

Is it free?

Yes. Build your personal library for free, no credit card required.
